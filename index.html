Chapter I

The idea

WoW: Forever, the new Classic+ version, was in beta, and I had access. I wanted an addon that makes the lore easy to follow: it knows which quests you have done, sums up the story so far, and reads the quests aloud.

All of it turned out to be possible, with one catch. Addons run in a sandbox without internet access, so the voices can't be made while you play. They have to be rendered ahead of time and shipped with the addon.

  • Track completed quests (the game already tells addons this)
  • Story recaps, written ahead of time
  • Voiceover, rendered ahead of time and bundled
Chapter II

The first build

The beta install turned out to live in _classic_beta_, client 1.60.1, Interface 16001. Since WoW: Forever quests are new content, the first version recorded quest text in game and voiced it afterwards on the PC, with the free Kokoro voice model running on the RTX 3080.

The very first line it spoke was a made-up one, just to hear if it worked.

Chapter III

A level 8 paladin

My test character, a paladin, was already level 8, and none of that history showed up. The fix: the client lists every quest a character has completed, so the journal filled in everything done before the addon was installed.

Chapter IV

Voiced from the start, for everyone

I didn't want anyone to type commands: the addon should have voiceover from the get-go. That changed the whole design. Instead of recording first, the classic quests came from the open cMaNGOS database, which has all 4,245 of them with their original text and who gives them. Each NPC's model tells their race and gender, so Coleman Farthing gets a Forsaken man's voice and Deathguard Linnea a Forsaken woman's.

The voices ship as one addon per zone, so players only download what they need. New WoW: Forever quests still come from recordings, which is how Wowhead gets its data too.

A line I didn't cross. Wowhead has the new quest text, but it is their collected data and Blizzard's writing. Scraping it would break their terms and put the addon at risk of a takedown. Chronicle collects the text the same way Wowhead's own client does: from the game, while people play.
Chapter V

Writing the story

The story recaps were written zone by zone to a strict brief: spoiler-safe, in the second person, never copying the quest text, and written to be read aloud. A validator checked every one. Levels 1 to 30 came first, then the rest of the game, including dungeons, raids, battlegrounds, class quests and holidays.

Chapter VI

The hunt for the right voice

Kokoro was fast but flat. My first reaction: "It sounds like an orc, but there's a robotic reverb on it." The pitch trick causing the reverb was replaced, and then three engines went head to head on the same nine characters: an orc, a tauren, two Forsaken, a dwarf, a night elf, a gnome, a goblin and a troll.

Chapter VII

The long render

Qwen sounded best and was also the slowest. About 70 hours of speech had to be rendered on one gaming GPU, and the first attempts went wrong in instructive ways.

  • Batches were half empty, because each voice was rendered separately. Fix: mix voices in one batch.
  • Long lines overflowed the 10 GB of video memory and crawled through system RAM. Fix: batches capped by text length.
  • The build died whenever its window closed. Fix: a Windows scheduled task that resumes by itself.
  • It kept the GPU when the game started. Fix: a watchdog that saves and steps aside within ten seconds, for any WoW client.
  • Console windows kept flashing on screen. "Was a bit annoying XD." Fix: every helper now runs without a window.

Chapter X

  • 1,921 lines NPCs say out loud in the world, from guards to Hogger, matched to who says them. They never talk over quest dialogue.
  • Books, letters and plaques read aloud, page by page
  • Your own character reacting after some turn-ins, in a voice for your race and gender
  • Story chapters as pins on the world map

  • Recordings swap automatically between party members, read by a strict parser that never runs anything it receives

Chapter XI

Checking the neighbours

A look at what else exists turned up several voice addons, some made for WoW: Forever in the same week, and a few lore journals. None of them combine the two: voiced quests without any story, or story journals without any voice. Nobody else narrates your own progress, voices greetings, world speech and books, or records new quests and shares them with the party.

The name is taken. A popular addon called Lorekeeper already exists on CurseForge, collecting in-game book texts. Two addons can't share a folder name, so I renamed the project: Chronicle in game, with the folder name ForeverChronicle so it can't collide with the other addons that use the word. The journal data, recordings and all 42 finished voice packs carried over.
Chapter XII

The night shift

While I slept and the PC kept rendering, the whole addon got a careful code review, looking for anything that would only break in the game.

  • Privacy: letters written by other players use the same reading window as books. They are now never recorded, shared or read aloud.
  • Sharing now sends only new WoW: Forever content, not classic quests that are merely waiting in the render queue
  • Recording of unknown greetings, books and NPC speech is capped, so saved data can't grow without end
  • The Characters tab could mix up a person's quests because list positions and quest IDs shared one table
  • A problem in the talking head can no longer leave the voice stuck in a "talking" state
  • /lore status shows memory use, and other voice addons get a one-time heads-up
Chapter XIII

Getting ready for the beta

Only about five quests had been played through end to end, so the first public version will be a beta: 1.0.0-beta.1, uploaded as CurseForge's "Beta" type. The name "Chronicle" is already crowded on CurseForge, so the project page will be called Chronicle: Voices of Azeroth. All voices together are too big for one download, so it ships as four projects: the addon itself (about 5 MB), Voices of Kalimdor (about 210 MB), Voices of the Eastern Kingdoms (about 250 MB) and Books & Adventures (about 200 MB). The CurseForge app installs the voice projects automatically with the addon, so voice still works from the start. The project page, changelog, readme, GPL license and a step-by-step upload checklist are ready.

A second review, this time thinking of strangers rather than friends, found the biggest issue in party sync. It accepted messages from anyone on any channel, including whispers from people outside your group, and stored whatever arrived. Nothing could run as code, but a bad record could break the journal every session and spread to everyone you grouped with afterwards.

  • Party sync now only accepts data from people in your party. Every record is rebuilt field by field from an allow-list, with size limits.
  • Party sync sends slowly enough for the server's message limit and resends pieces that were dropped. It only passes on your own recordings, never ones it received from others.
  • A party member sharing a quest with you is no longer recorded as its quest giver (a privacy fix)
  • Female characters could hit a Lua error on some book pages
  • NPC greetings no longer cut off the turn-in line, your character's reaction or the recap
  • On non-English game clients, Chronicle stays quiet instead of recording text in that language
  • Existing characters no longer send thousands of quest lookups to the server at every login
  • A welcome message on first login; a note when a zone's voice pack is missing or turned off; /chronicle as a second command; /lore report notes now go out with /lore share
Chapter XIV

Tidying up, and counting what's new

The AddOns list had grown to about 46 Chronicle entries, one per zone. The zones were folded into three packs instead: Voices of Kalimdor, Voices of the Eastern Kingdoms and Books & Adventures, each with its own icon. Every zone keeps its own folder inside its pack. All 10,786 finished recordings were moved, not rendered again, and the render picked up exactly where it had paused.

A small scanner then asked the game for the title of every quest ID in WoW: Forever's range. It found 728 quests that were not in Classic. The game only reveals titles this way, not the spoken text, so voicing them still depends on players opening them. But now there is a checklist to measure that against.

Back in game after work, I found the rough edges. Long quest text was cut off in the talking head, and the "New in WoW: Forever" marker hid behind the portrait. The journal also listed Tirisfal Glades twice.

  • Subtitles now page through long text in step with the voice
  • The "New in WoW: Forever · voice coming soon" marker sits where the Listen button would be
  • One heading per zone in the journal, and "Met in…" under new NPCs
  • Characters: drag to turn the model, scroll to zoom, the model talks while "Hear them" plays (their greeting first), clickable dealings that open the quest's chapter, and a list grouped by zone
  • Memory measured in game: 9.6 MB, light next to the big questing addons
Chapter XV

The casting rounds

Testing in Tirisfal, Gordo turned out to speak with a soft girl's voice. Every undead without a playable race got the same "restless ghost" voice, and hundreds of monsters, from harpies to ogres, got a generic adventurer. So the voices are now cast by each NPC's 3D model: abominations, ogres, gnolls, kobolds, harpies, dryads, kids and high elves each have their own voice family, and the model also tells the voice when a nameless NPC is a woman.

Then the bigger question: many lines sounded flat. I ran four listening rounds on the same quest lines. New voices designed per character came out higher and less manly than the ones I liked. A paid premium model acted better and said names better, but not by enough to be worth it for now. Voices for every race and sex, sound effects per race to make them less human: some good, some robotic. In the end the answer was simple. I kept the voices I already liked and only changed how they are cloned, so the model phrases each line itself instead of copying a flat reading. The gnome men, which sounded like women, and the abominations got new voices of their own.

Chapter XVI

A new cast

The free voice model always made a human doing a voice: the orc was a man putting on a deep voice, the gnome sounded like a girl. So I built a small casting studio to listen through every voice in one place, and tried ElevenLabs' voice design on the four characters that bothered me most: an orc, a dwarf, a Forsaken and Gordo. It was a different league. Orcs sounded like orcs.

Having ElevenLabs read all 28,000 lines would have cost well over a thousand dollars. So ElevenLabs only designs the voices, and the free model on my PC clones them and speaks every line. To my ears that came close enough to the real thing. The whole cast, 93 voices for every race and sex, the monsters and the high elves, took about 15,000 credits. ElevenLabs won't design children's voices, so the kids keep theirs, and the narrator and my gnome stay as they were. Every line is now being re-voiced with the new cast, and the beta goes out once levels 1 to 30 are done.

Chapter XVII

The first public beta

At some point I stopped waiting for the new cast to reach every line. It's a beta. So the rule became simple: as soon as every line from level 1 to 30 had a voice, old cast or new, it ships. The last few hundred were long book pages, and they finished overnight while I had Blender and Unity open for my other project.

This morning all 15,871 lines had a voice. I built the release, checked that every line in the packs had its audio file, and uploaded Chronicle 1.0.0-beta.1 to CurseForge: the addon itself and three voice packs, Kalimdor, the Eastern Kingdoms and Books & Adventures. They're waiting for CurseForge's approval now. Once the packs are approved, the addon will install them automatically. Meanwhile the PC keeps rendering levels 31 and up, and the new cast keeps replacing the old voices for the next update.

  • Every line from level 1 to 30 voiced: 15,871 of 15,871
  • Chronicle 1.0.0-beta.1 uploaded to CurseForge, with three voice packs (about 870 MB)

Chapter XVIII

After launch

The first reports were all the same: people installed Chronicle and heard nothing. The CurseForge app had skipped the voice packs. It took a while to find out why. The app only installs dependencies that are marked as a release, and all my files were marked beta. Once that was fixed, one click on Chronicle brought all four along. For anyone who still ends up without them, Chronicle now shows a popup that says exactly what to install.

I posted it on Reddit, and the replies were better than I hoped. People liked the story side most. A few don't like AI voices, which is fair, so there's now a "journal only" switch that turns every voice off. Another addon developer offered to work together, and I opened a Discord for bug reports and for sharing recordings of the new Forever quests.

Two things got a lot better in the voices. Long lines used to be rendered in pieces and glued together, so the mood could change halfway through a sentence. Now every line is read in one go. And the narrator got a new voice: an epic fantasy storyteller that finally says "Executor" the way the game does.

Chapter XIX

Listening to the players

With real people playing, the bug reports started to come from the game itself instead of from my own testing. Some of the best ones I found while playing myself.

Pick up quests from two NPCs in a row and the second one talked right over the first. Click a quest while the NPC was still greeting you and the greeting was cut off mid-sentence. Now whoever is talking gets to finish, and the next line waits its turn.

Quest text is full of little stage directions, like "<Kaltunk looks you over.>" or "<snort>", and the voices were reading them out loud. They're silent now, and the subtitles show them in grey so you can tell what's said from what's done.

Then there were the voices that just sounded wrong. Mai'ah, the troll mage trainer in Durotar, sounded more like a gnome, and so did 38 other troll women who shared her voice. And the gnome men, Felix Whindlebolt among them, sounded like they were talking from inside a pipe. Both got a new voice.

Chapter XX

Reading it all again

The recaps, chapter intros and character bios are what people mention most, so they had to be right. Every one of them, more than five thousand texts, was checked against the quest text from the game. Then again. Then the levels 1 to 30 that beta players hear right now got a third look.

About one in thirty had something wrong. Kernobee was called a goblin, and Dark Cleric Beryl stood in the wrong village. Rexxar's key opened the wrong lair. A recap claimed Ello Ebonlocke was Abercrombie's old friend, when Ello has never heard of him, and that's a hint you shouldn't spoil. Some recaps borrowed a detail from the next quest in the chain, and the Ahn'Qiraj armor quests all ended on the same tagline. Around four hundred texts were fixed in total.

The checks turned up a few voice problems too. The Fallen Hero in the Swamp of Sorrows, an orc spirit, was listed in the game data as a human woman, and was voiced like one. Two goblins in Orgrimmar were listed as an orc and a troll. They all have the right voices now.

The options got sorted into sections, and each kind of voice has its own switch, so you can keep only the narrator if that's what you like. That one came straight from a player on the Discord.

  • Every recap, intro and bio checked against the quest text
  • Fixed the Fallen Hero's voice, and two goblins voiced as an orc and a troll
  • Options in sections, a switch for every voice
  • Quests with one copy per starting zone no longer all show up in the journal
  • The Options button waits until the fight is over
